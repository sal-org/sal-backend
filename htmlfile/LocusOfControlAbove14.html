About the Report:

The locus of control and related beliefs affect our cognition and behaviour and, as such, have a vital role to play across multiple areas of our lives. It is the extent to which we feel we have control over events that impact our life. It is a belief about whether the outcomes of our actions are contingent on what we do or on events outside our personal control.


What is your Locus of Control?

If you believe that you have control over what happens, you have an internal locus of control. If you believe that you have no control over what happens and that external variables are to blame, you have what is known as an external locus of control.


It is important to note that locus of control is a continuum. No one has a 100% external or internal locus of control. Instead, most people lie somewhere on the continuum between the two extremes.

Internal locus of control is when individuals who feel a high degree of control over the events and outcomes in their lives. Typically, they believe they are in control of their fate and that results reflect the amount of energy they exert. They tend to attribute their successes and failures to their own actions, decisions, and efforts rather than external factors. They are more likely to take initiative, set goals, and actively work towards achieving them. These individuals often have a sense of empowerment and confidence in their ability to influence outcomes.



While an internal locus of control is typically beneficial, too much can have potential downsides, such as being overly self-reliant and failing to ask for help. Research suggests that those who perceive their control as internal are sometimes less able to deal with workplace stress.


In such scenarios, working with a counsellor can help one to maintain a balanced perspective and help mitigate the potential downsides of an extreme internal locus of control while still harnessing its positive aspects for mental health and well-being.
