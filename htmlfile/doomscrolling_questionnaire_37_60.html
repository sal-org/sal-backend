About the Report:

The locus of control and related beliefs affect our cognition and behaviour and, as such, have a vital role to play across multiple areas of our lives. It is the extent to which we feel we have control over events that impact our life. It is a belief about whether the outcomes of our actions are contingent on what we do or on events outside our personal control.


What is Doomscrolling?

  • It is the act of spending an excessive amount of time reading large quantities of user- generated content or news, particularly negative digital news.
  • Most people in modern times do not realize that they are even seeking negative information. Social media algorithms heed the content users engage in and display posts similar in nature, which can aid in the act of doomscrolling.

Health professionals have advised that doomscrolling can negatively impact existing mental health issues. While the overall impact that doomscrolling has on people may vary, it can often make one feel anxious, stressed, fearful, depressed, and isolated.

What is your Doomscrolling Scale?

This scale is designed to serve as a valuable tool for individuals to self-assess their doomscrolling tendencies and take proactive steps towards fostering healthier digital habits.

The questionnaire contains questions about the extent to which the following thoughts, feelings or actions are characteristic of you. This interpretation is used to understand an individual's level of engagement in this behaviour, its relationship to other psychological factors, and its severity, with insights into its potential impact on stress, anxiety, and other mental health conditions.

Suggestions:

You may try gentle strategies, such as mindful breaks, turning off late-night alerts, or replacing news-scrolling with a calming activity. With steady practice, these small changes can restore balance and leave you feeling more present and energized.

  • Turn off push notifications for news and social apps to reduce constant pull.
  • Try a ‘no-scroll’ window each day, such as the first 30 minutes after waking.
  • Keep a list of quick, uplifting alternatives (walk, music, chat with a friend).
  • Protect your sleep by setting a clear digital cut-off time in the evening

In such scenarios, working with a counsellor can help by increasing awareness of the habit and its emotional impact, identifying personal triggers, and teaching practical strategies to set healthier boundaries with news use. They can guide by preventing the behaviour from escalating further, while supporting overall emotional balance and well-being.
