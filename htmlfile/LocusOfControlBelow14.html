About the Report:

The locus of control and related beliefs affect our cognition and behaviour and, as such, have a vital role to play across multiple areas of our lives. It is the extent to which we feel we have control over events that impact our life. It is a belief about whether the outcomes of our actions are contingent on what we do or on events outside our personal control.


What is your Locus of Control?

If you believe that you have control over what happens, you have an internal locus of control. If you believe that you have no control over what happens and that external variables are to blame, you have what is known as an external locus of control.



It is important to note that locus of control is a continuum. No one has a 100% external or internal locus of control. Instead, most people lie somewhere on the continuum between the two extremes.

Inference:

Your overall score range is below 14, based on the assessment. Your score suggests you have an external locus of control. Having an external locus of control can be a good thing—particularly when a situation poses a threat to self-esteem or is genuinely outside of a person's control.



External locus of control is when individuals perceive that external factors such as luck, fate, or other people have a greater influence over their lives. They may feel that they have little control over their circumstances and that their actions have limited impact on the outcomes. They may be more prone to feelings of helplessness, resignation, or dependence on external sources for guidance or direction. These individuals might be less likely to take initiative or feel motivated to change their situation due to a belief that external forces largely determine their fate.



While an external locus of control is typically beneficial, too less can have potential downsides. Research suggests that those who perceive their control as external may frequently feel hopeless or powerless in the face of difficult situations. This sense of helplessness can lead to feelings of despair, hopelessness and depression. Feeling powerless to change one’s circumstances can lead to social withdrawal and isolation. This can exacerbate mental health issues and hinder personal growth and resilience.


Seeking support from family, friends, or mental health professionals can be beneficial.
